Dark matter at galactic scales

A data-driven approach to the overall geometry of dark matter haloes at galactic scales. Rather than assuming a halo profile and fitting to it, these papers ask what the observations on their own can say about the shape of the mass distribution and the nature of dark matter, using galactic rotation curves, the torsion of stellar streams, and other kinematic tracers.

2026

  1. New Astron.
    No evidence for Keplerian taper of far-out galactic rotation in the SPARC galaxy database
    Adriana Bariego-Quintana and Felipe J. Llanes-Estrada
    New Astronomy, 2026
    Abs DOI arXiv Bib HTML

    Statistical analysis of 175 rotation curves from the SPARC database, testing whether galactic rotation velocities taper off in a Keplerian way at large radii. The data are compatible with the null hypothesis of no taper and no decline; the Milky Way instead shows a decline of about 20%, suggesting it may not be typical.

2024

  1. ICWIP
    How Stellar Stream Torsion may reveal aspherical Dark Matter Haloes
    Adriana Bariego-Quintana
    Proceedings of the 8th International Conference on Women in Physics (ICWIP 2023), 2024
    Abs arXiv Bib

    Stellar streams would lie on a plane if the dark matter halo’s gravitational field were spherically symmetric. An aspherical field torsions them out of that plane, which makes torsion a direct probe of halo shape. Milky Way streams show torsion well above the spherical expectation.

  3. A&A
    The torsion of stellar streams and the overall shape of galactic gravity’s source
    Adriana Bariego-Quintana and Felipe J. Llanes-Estrada
    Astronomy & Astrophysics, 2024
    Abs DOI arXiv Bib HTML

    Flat rotation curves are naturally explained by elongated dark matter distributions. We introduce the torsion of a stellar stream as a local observable that measures deviation from planarity, and show that central forces produce negligible torsion while distorted haloes generate measurable values. Milky Way streams at large galactocentric distances show torsion much larger than expected for a spherical bulb alone.
